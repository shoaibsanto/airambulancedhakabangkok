Air Ambulance Service Dhaka to Bangkok

End-to-end ICU air ambulance, medical evacuation and commercial medical escort services moving critically ill and injured patients safely from Dhaka, Bangladesh to leading Bangkok hospitals — fully monitored, doctor-led and available 24/7.

What We Provide

What Complete Medical Flight Solutions Do We Offer?

Every transfer is a single coordinated chain of care — from the patient's bedside in Dhaka to admission at a hospital in Bangkok. Our team handles the air ambulance, the flight doctor and paramedic, the medical equipment, the ground ambulances on both ends, and the documentation, so families can focus on their loved one rather than logistics.

1. ICU Air Ambulance (Charter Jet)

A dedicated air ambulance charter jet configured as a flying intensive care unit. The aircraft carries a transport ventilator, multi-parameter monitor, infusion pumps and oxygen reserves, staffed by a critical care flight doctor and paramedic. Ideal for unstable patients, ventilator-dependent cases and time-critical medical evacuation where a non-stop, controlled environment is essential between Dhaka and Bangkok.

2. Commercial Airline Medical Escort

For stable patients who do not need a charter jet, we arrange a commercial medical escort on scheduled flights. A trained flight doctor or nurse travels alongside the patient with portable oxygen, medications and monitoring, plus pre-arranged stretcher or extra-legroom seating. This is a cost-effective alternative for routine repatriation and recovery transfers to Bangkok.

3. Ground Ambulance & Bed-to-Bed Transfer

True bed-to-bed transfer means the patient never has to be handed off into uncertainty. We dispatch a fully equipped ground ambulance to lift the patient from their hospital bed or home in Dhaka, transport them to the aircraft, and a second ambulance receives them in Bangkok for the final leg to the admitting hospital — one continuous, monitored journey under our care.

4. Medical Repatriation & Return

When a patient stabilises after treatment in Bangkok, we manage their safe return to Dhaka or onward to home. Our repatriation service covers post-operative patients, accident survivors and chronic cases, coordinating discharge summaries, fit-to-fly clearance and continuity of care so the homecoming is just as carefully supervised as the outbound flight.

5. Neonatal, Paediatric & Cardiac Critical Transfer

Specialised transfers for the most fragile patients. Our neonatal and paediatric service uses a transport incubator and dedicated specialists for newborns and children, while cardiac critical care transfers carry advanced monitoring, defibrillation and IABP-ready protocols. These complex cases are escorted by experienced critical care teams trained for high-acuity medical evacuation.

6. Visa, Permits & Documentation Assistance

Cross-border medical evacuation depends on paperwork being right the first time. We assist with Thai medical visas, hospital invitation letters, landing and overflight permits, customs clearance for medical equipment, and insurance liaison. Our coordinators work directly with Bangkok hospitals including Bumrungrad International Hospital to confirm admission before departure.

ICU Air Ambulance Equipment

What Equipment Is on Board an ICU Air Ambulance Flight?

Understanding exactly what medical technology travels with your patient is important when choosing an air ambulance provider. Our ICU-configured charter jet carries hospital-grade life-support systems, not basic first-aid kits. Here is a detailed breakdown of every major piece of equipment on board every flight from Dhaka to Bangkok and why it matters for critical patient safety.

Transport Ventilator

The transport ventilator is the centrepiece of any ICU air ambulance. Unlike portable ventilators used for short-term manual bagging, our aviation-grade transport ventilator supports full mechanical ventilation for the entire duration of the flight — typically 2.5 to 3.5 hours in the air between Dhaka and Bangkok. It provides multiple modes including volume control, pressure control, SIMV and CPAP, allowing the flight doctor to tailor ventilation to the patient's specific condition, whether that is post-cardiac arrest, acute respiratory failure, severe stroke or post-operative ventilator dependence. The device operates on aircraft power and has internal battery backup. Adjustable tidal volumes, respiratory rates and PEEP settings let the flight doctor maintain precise PaO₂ and PaCO₂ targets at cruise altitude, where the reduced cabin pressure environment (equivalent to 6,000–8,000 feet) can affect respiratory physiology. For neonatal and paediatric cases, the same ventilator supports smaller tidal volumes and specialised paediatric circuits, eliminating the need for a separate device.

Cardiac Monitor & Multi-Parameter Monitoring

Our multi-parameter cardiac monitor provides continuous, simultaneous visibility of all vital signs throughout the transfer. The screen displays 5-lead or 12-lead ECG with real-time ST-segment analysis, which is critical for detecting evolving myocardial infarction or arrhythmia in cardiac patients. Pulse oximetry (SpO₂) gives moment-by-moment oxygen saturation, while end-tidal CO₂ (EtCO₂) capnography confirms correct endotracheal tube placement and tracks ventilation efficacy — indispensable for ventilated patients. Non-invasive blood pressure (NIBP) measurement cycles automatically at set intervals, and where invasive arterial lines are in place from the sending ICU, the monitor connects directly to display beat-by-beat arterial waveform and blood pressure. Temperature probes can be connected to detect hyperthermia or hypothermia during transfer. Every data point streams and can be recorded, giving the receiving Bangkok hospital a complete physiological timeline from departure to arrival — a detail that specialists find invaluable for immediate clinical decision-making on admission.

Defibrillator with External Pacing

The defibrillator on board is a fully featured biphasic unit capable of synchronised DC cardioversion, unsynchronised defibrillation and transcutaneous (external) cardiac pacing. This capability is essential for cardiac emergency patients, post-arrest transfers and patients with unstable arrhythmias. If a patient deteriorates into ventricular fibrillation or pulseless ventricular tachycardia during the flight, the flight doctor can deliver immediate defibrillation. For patients with symptomatic bradycardia or heart block who have not yet had a temporary pacemaker inserted, external transcutaneous pacing provides a life-saving bridge during the 3–4 hours in transit from Dhaka to Bangkok. Patients being transferred for elective cardiac surgery or post-PCI monitoring benefit equally from having this capability on standby throughout the journey, even if it is never activated. For the most complex cardiac cases, our team can also accommodate an intra-aortic balloon pump (IABP) on the aircraft when required, subject to aircraft type and pre-mission planning.

Infusion & Syringe Pumps

Critically ill patients almost always require multiple intravenous infusions running simultaneously — vasopressors to maintain blood pressure, sedation and analgesia, antibiotic drips, anticoagulation, electrolyte corrections and specialised medications such as insulin infusions, amiodarone or magnesium. Our air ambulance carries multiple infusion pumps and syringe drivers, each independently programmable to deliver drugs at precise flow rates in millilitres per hour. Aviation-grade pumps are secured to the aircraft stretcher frame and continue operating during turbulence without flow interruption — a critical distinction from bedside hospital pumps that may alarm and stall. The flight doctor pre-calculates all drug rates and prepares syringes and bags before departure, carries reserve volumes for every infusion, and can add or switch medications in flight. This capability means the patient does not need to be weaned off life-sustaining medications simply because they are being transported — continuity of ICU pharmacological support is maintained from the Dhaka hospital bed to the Bangkok hospital bed without interruption.

Medical Oxygen & Suction

We carry medical-grade oxygen in calculated reserves sufficient for the entire mission including ground legs, planned flight time and a safety buffer for delays or diversions. For patients on high-flow oxygen therapy, ventilators or CPAP, the oxygen supply is managed and monitored by the flight doctor throughout. Our portable suction unit handles airway secretions, which is critical for unconscious or intubated patients, those recovering from aspiration pneumonia, and post-surgical patients with tracheostomy. A portable blood gas analyser allows the flight doctor to check arterial pH, PaO₂, PaCO₂, lactate, sodium and potassium levels mid-flight — results available in minutes — so that ventilator settings and medications can be adjusted based on real data rather than estimation. This on-board laboratory capability sets a professional ICU air ambulance apart from a basic medevac service.

Emergency Drug Box & Intubation Kit

Our comprehensive emergency drug box is stocked for the full range of in-flight medical emergencies, not only the patient's presenting diagnosis. It includes cardiac emergency drugs (adrenaline, atropine, amiodarone, adenosine, dopamine, noradrenaline), neurological medications (phenytoin, lorazepam, mannitol, dexamethasone), anaesthetic agents (propofol, ketamine, midazolam), reversal agents, antibiotics, antiemetics, antihistamines and emergency airway adjuncts. The intubation kit carries laryngoscopes, video laryngoscope capability, a range of endotracheal tube sizes from neonatal to adult, a surgical airway kit and a difficult airway bag. All drugs are checked and restocked before every mission. For neonatal and paediatric transfers, drug doses are pre-calculated by weight in a separate colour-coded paediatric emergency reference system carried by the flight paramedic, ensuring accurate dosing without delay if the child's condition changes in flight.

How It Works

Step-by-Step Booking Process: From Your First Call to Bangkok Admission

Arranging an ICU air ambulance from Dhaka to Bangkok does not need to be complicated. Our coordination team manages every step in parallel, so the logistics never slow down the patient's transfer. Here is exactly what happens from the moment you call us to the moment the patient is admitted to a Bangkok hospital.

Step 1 — Call or Contact Our 24/7 Medical Flight Desk

The process begins the moment you call 01716-960770 or send an enquiry via our contact form. Our flight coordinator answers immediately, 24 hours a day, seven days a week, including public holidays. In the first five minutes we will ask for the patient's current location in Dhaka, their primary diagnosis, the name of the sending hospital and doctor, current vital signs or ICU status if available, and the preferred receiving hospital in Bangkok. This first call is free of obligation — we are gathering information to give you an accurate assessment, not committing you to a booking.

Step 2 — Medical Assessment and Aircraft Recommendation

Once we have the patient's details, our in-house medical team (including flight doctors with critical care experience) reviews the clinical picture and recommends the right level of service. Some patients require a full ICU charter jet with a flight doctor, paramedic and the complete life-support package described above. Others — stable post-operative patients, ambulatory patients requiring medication and monitoring, or those on low-flow oxygen — can be transferred safely via a commercial medical escort, which is significantly more cost-effective. We will give you an honest recommendation based on the medical reports, not the option that generates the highest fee. If you can send us hospital notes, discharge summaries or recent lab and imaging reports by WhatsApp or email, we can refine the recommendation further and give a more precise quote.

Step 3 — Aircraft Sourcing and Bangkok Hospital Admission Coordination

Once you confirm, both of the following happen simultaneously: our operations team sources and places the appropriate aircraft on standby — either from our own fleet or from partner operators on the Dhaka–Bangkok route — and our hospital coordination team contacts the international patient desk at the receiving Bangkok hospital. We work directly with Bumrungrad International Hospital, BNH Hospital, Samitivej Hospital and Bangkok Hospital to pre-arrange admission, confirm bed availability and brief the specialist team. For cardiac cases, we alert the cardiac catheterisation lab or the cardiothoracic surgical unit. For stroke patients, we notify the neurosurgical team and the stroke unit. For cancer patients, we liaise with the oncology department. This pre-admission briefing means the patient is not waiting in an ambulance outside a hospital on arrival — the clinical team is ready at the door.

Step 4 — Permits, Documentation and Pre-Departure Preparation

Flight permits, overflight clearances and landing slots are applied for and confirmed before the aircraft is ever loaded. Our team prepares the patient's travel documents including Thai medical visa support letters, fit-to-fly medical certification signed by the flight doctor, and insurance pre-authorisation documentation if required. The flight doctor and paramedic review the patient's full medical history, prepare the drug box, verify the equipment, brief the ground ambulance crew in Dhaka and establish direct communication with the receiving hospital team in Bangkok. All of this happens while the family is simply waiting for departure — they receive regular updates from a single point of contact.

Step 5 — Ground Ambulance Pickup from the Dhaka Hospital or Home

Our fully equipped ground ambulance, staffed by a trained medical crew, arrives at the patient's location — whether that is a Dhaka hospital ICU, a private clinic or the patient's home. The flight doctor is on board or at the ramp to supervise the patient pickup. The patient is transferred to our medical stretcher, connected to the portable monitoring and infusion systems that will travel with them onto the aircraft, and transported to Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport. The entire pickup-to-ramp process is managed by our crew — families do not need to arrange separate transport or liaise with the airport independently.

Step 6 — ICU Air Ambulance Flight, Dhaka to Bangkok

The patient is loaded directly onto the aircraft from the ambulance at the ramp — no waiting in a terminal. The aircraft is configured as a flying intensive care unit. All life-support connections are maintained throughout boarding. The flight from Dhaka to Bangkok takes approximately 2.5 to 3 hours in the air. The flight doctor and paramedic monitor all vital parameters continuously, administer medications, manage the ventilator and document the clinical record for handover. The family of the patient may travel on the same aircraft in most charter configurations, seated away from the medical area. Real-time updates are relayed to the ground coordination team, who pass them to the receiving hospital and the family members in Bangkok.

Step 7 — Bangkok Ground Ambulance and Hospital Admission

On landing at Suvarnabhumi or Don Mueang Airport in Bangkok, a pre-arranged ground ambulance and our Bangkok ground coordinator meet the aircraft at the ramp. The patient is transferred from the aircraft directly into the ground ambulance without going through the public terminal. The flight doctor completes a formal clinical handover — verbal and written — to the receiving hospital's intensive care or specialist team, including vital sign trends, medications administered in flight, any in-flight events, and current status. The patient is then admitted directly to the agreed ward or ICU. Our role ends only when the patient is safely in the care of the Bangkok medical team and the family is settled. Total elapsed time from departure in Dhaka to hospital admission in Bangkok is typically four to six hours, depending on ground transport distances at both ends.

Why Bangkok

Why Do Bangladesh Patients Choose Bangkok for Medical Treatment?

Bangladesh patients and families make the decision to travel to Bangkok for medical treatment based on a combination of world-class hospital quality, specialist availability, cost relative to alternatives, and the established track record of Thai hospitals in treating international patients from South Asia. Here is what drives that choice for most families we work with.

Bumrungrad International Hospital — A Global Standard of Care in Bangkok

Bumrungrad International Hospital is consistently ranked among Asia's top hospitals and sees more than one million patients per year from over 190 countries — one of the largest international patient volumes of any hospital in the world. For Bangladeshi patients, Bumrungrad is the most recognised and most requested destination. Its Heart Institute offers 24/7 interventional cardiology including primary PCI for STEMI, cardiac surgery, electrophysiology and a dedicated cardiac ICU. The Neuroscience Centre operates a 24/7 stroke team with interventional neuroradiology and neurosurgery capability. Bumrungrad's oncology centre — the Horizon Regional Cancer Centre — provides chemotherapy, radiation oncology, robotic surgery and targeted therapy. The hospital has a dedicated Bangladesh patient coordinator on site and Bengali-speaking interpreters, which removes the language and administrative barrier that families often fear most. We have direct working relationships with Bumrungrad's international patient department and coordinate admission ahead of every patient's arrival.

BNH Hospital — Personalised Care at Lower Cost

BNH Hospital in central Bangkok (Bang Rak district) is a JCI-accredited private hospital that offers a more personalised, less crowded alternative to Bumrungrad for patients who need specialist-level care in a quieter setting. BNH has strong departments in obstetrics, gynaecology, orthopaedics, general surgery and internal medicine. For Bangladeshi patients who require planned procedures rather than emergency admission — or for post-operative recovery where round-the-clock monitoring is needed but critical care intensity is lower — BNH provides a high standard of care at a noticeably lower daily room and procedure cost than the larger tertiary hospitals. We transfer patients to BNH for a range of elective and semi-elective cases and maintain direct contact with their international patient services team.

Samitivej Hospital — Paediatric and Women's Health Specialist

Samitivej Sukhumvit Hospital in Bangkok is particularly well regarded for its paediatric hospital (Samitivej Children's Hospital) and its women's health and fertility services. For Bangladeshi families transferring children with complex neonatal or paediatric conditions, Samitivej's dedicated children's hospital — one of the largest paediatric facilities in Southeast Asia — offers specialist neonatologists, paediatric surgeons, paediatric cardiologists and paediatric neurologists. For adult patients with obstetric emergencies, gynaecological cancers or women's health conditions requiring specialist surgical care, Samitivej's women's services are a frequent choice. Our neonatal transport team has specific experience moving newborns in transport incubators from Dhaka to Samitivej, coordinating with their NICU team for admission before departure.

Specialist Availability That Remains Limited in Bangladesh

A key driver of medical evacuation from Bangladesh to Bangkok is the limited in-country availability of certain medical specialties. Procedures such as transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR), mechanical thrombectomy for acute ischaemic stroke within the therapeutic window, complex robotic oncological surgery, liver transplant and bone marrow transplant are either unavailable or available only at a small number of centres with long waiting times in Bangladesh. Bangkok hospitals can schedule these procedures rapidly — often within days of arrival — with specialists who perform them at high volume. For time-critical conditions where a treatment window exists (acute MI, stroke, transplant candidacy), the ability to access Bangkok's specialist services within hours rather than weeks can be life-changing.

Cost Comparison: Bangkok vs India for Bangladeshi Patients

India — specifically Chennai, Kolkata and Delhi — has historically been the most common international medical destination for Bangladeshi patients. Bangkok competes directly with India for the same patient population and, for many procedures, is now cost-competitive or cheaper on a total-cost basis when all factors are included. Thai hospital procedure fees for cardiac surgery, neurosurgery and oncology treatment are broadly comparable to top-tier private hospitals in Chennai or Delhi. Flight time from Dhaka to Bangkok (approximately 2.5–3 hours) is similar to or shorter than many Dhaka–India routes. Bangkok hospitals have more capacity to schedule international patients quickly, and the quality of facilities at Bumrungrad, BNH and Samitivej is consistently above the average private Indian hospital catering to medical tourists. Visa processing for Thailand is also faster and simpler for Bangladeshi nationals in many cases. When families calculate the real total cost — treatment, accommodation, food, family members' travel and visa, and the time the patient is away — Bangkok is often comparable to or less expensive than India for equivalent care. For emergency transfers specifically, where time is the priority, Bangkok's combination of proximity, specialist availability and established Bangladeshi patient infrastructure makes it the natural first choice for families we advise.

Why Experience with the Dhaka–Bangkok Route Matters

Not every air ambulance provider is equally experienced on the Dhaka–Bangkok corridor. This specific route has its own logistical characteristics: the permit environment at Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport, the coordination protocols with Bangkok's Suvarnabhumi and Don Mueang airports, the direct relationships with the international patient departments at Bumrungrad, BNH and Samitivej, and the practical experience of managing Bangladeshi families through a stressful cross-border medical transfer for the first time. Our team has operated exclusively on this route for years. The guides on this site are written from that accumulated experience — not from generic air ambulance industry knowledge — and every family we help benefits from the specific contacts, workflows and lessons built on that single corridor.
